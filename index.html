About

I study how deployed multilingual LLM systems drift from benchmark behavior once real-world feedback loops take over. My current research formalizes this as Feedback-Induced Distribution Shift (FIDS) — three related failure patterns I observed directly while directing AI and data programs at Google across 30+ languages and 100+ product markets — and proposes reinforcement-learning-based adaptation strategies to address them.

I'm also developing a second paper on the gap between engagement and accuracy in LLM-mediated search for high-stakes queries.

Before moving into research, I spent a decade in AI program leadership at Google and in Microsoft-adjacent roles, and I currently lead product and technology at an AI health-tech startup. My long-term goal is to found a research-driven AI company.

The FIDS Taxonomy

Three failure patterns that emerge when human feedback loops govern multilingual LLM deployment, formalized with propositions and proofs in the FIDS paper.

AME
Aggregate Masking Effect
Quality regressions in specific locales get averaged away by the dashboard-level metric leadership actually watches — the whole looks fine while parts quietly aren't.
ADL
Aspirational Deferral Lock
Once a metric crosses a "good enough" threshold, the underlying backlog gets deprioritized indefinitely — the target becomes the ceiling.
LRLC
Low-Resource Locale Collapse
Locales that start behind receive proportionally less correction each cycle, so the gap compounds round over round instead of closing.
